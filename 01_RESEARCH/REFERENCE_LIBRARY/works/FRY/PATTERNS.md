# FRY — competitive strategy transfer candidates

Twelve labeled strategic candidates, not twelve independently certified narrative techniques. Related IDs identify possible overlap; no claims of novelty, optimality, actual games or reader-tested effects. All sentences and blocks are original conceptual demonstrations, noncanonical. Use only in legal game situations; no hidden information, unverified effects or improper tournament conduct.

## FRY-S01 — Result–decision separation
Level: L6. Evidence: MUS-PREF 18–19; MUS-EARTH 28–30. Related: P18; M64.
Structure and inputs: Win/loss → reconstruct options available at decision → compare reasons and alternative explanations → choose one practice target.
Expected effect / use: A win may conceal a poor choice; a loss may follow a defensible one.
Original illustration: She put the winning game in the review pile, too.
Failure: Treating tournament record as a proof of mastery.
Acceptance check: Require known-state record; do not infer causation from outcome.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S02 — Contextual tool allocation
Level: L3. Evidence: MUS-EARTH 22–28; MUS-WIND 65–68. Related: M63; C01; C10.
Structure and inputs: Identify matchup goal → compare roles, resource costs and restrictions → assign a tool → retain fallback.
Expected effect / use: A familiar card can be wrong in a particular context.
Original illustration: The card had earned its slot against slow decks; tonight she questioned whether it belonged there.
Failure: Universal ranking based on rarity or preferred archetype.
Acceptance check: Verify deck legality and matchup evidence before any specific recommendation.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S03 — Wide-state scan
Level: L2. Evidence: MUS-WATER 31–33; MUS-FIRE 63; MUS-WIND 70. Related: P12; P13; B02.
Structure and inputs: Local decision → inspect score, board, available resources, pending effects and clock → reevaluate local choice.
Expected effect / use: Prevents fixation on a single exchange.
Original illustration: He checked the score slip before deciding which threat mattered.
Failure: Hero notices every hidden threat without evidence.
Acceptance check: Only public facts and POV-known information; unknown hand remains a range.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S04 — Initiative without mandatory aggression
Level: L3. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 52–53; YAG-KILLING 86–88. Related: P09; B02.
Structure and inputs: Define desired state → choose proactive pressure or reserved response → account for opponent reply → evaluate whether opponent must address objective.
Expected effect / use: Reactive play can shape decisions as effectively as an attack.
Original illustration: She had a plan for either answer, so she could afford to let him choose.
Failure: Assuming initiative means acting first or passing illegally.
Acceptance check: Check priority, available actions and legal timing; both players have agency.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S05 — Probe with bounded downside
Level: L3. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 56–57; YAG-KILLING 85–89. Related: P13; P18; B02.
Structure and inputs: Multiple plausible opponent plans → select legal low-cost information-producing action → observe actual reply → update range and next choice.
Expected effect / use: Information can justify a small concession; silence is ambiguous.
Original illustration: He tried the cheaper option first and waited to see whether she would spend a response.
Failure: Treating a pass as proof of an empty hand; illegal deception.
Acceptance check: Specify cost, alternative use and multiple compatible hidden states.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S06 — Interrupt enabling dependency
Level: L3. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 53–56; YAG-LIFE 99–104. Related: P09; P16.
Structure and inputs: Identify documented payoff dependency → verify legal interaction window → compare interruption now versus later → account for alternate line.
Expected effect / use: Disrupting a prerequisite can matter more than answering the final threat.
Original illustration: She had saved her answer for the payoff; in review, they examined whether the setup had been the better target.
Failure: Inventing an interruption window or assuming a single choke point.
Acceptance check: Source all effects and priority; record opponent alternatives; no optimality claim without evidence.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S07 — Change the axis of a stalled contest
Level: L3. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 56–57,61–63; MUS-WATER 40–41. Related: C01; P18.
Structure and inputs: Detect repeated low-value exchange → identify another legal objective or resource axis → compare switch cost → commit conditionally.
Expected effect / use: Varies the strategic problem without gratuitous novelty.
Original illustration: After two unproductive exchanges, he considered whether the other battlefield was worth contesting.
Failure: Changing plans just to surprise the audience.
Acceptance check: Alternative must serve scoring or another established objective; validate movement and costs if depicted.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S08 — Reassess after advantage
Level: L2. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 55–56,62; YAG-LIFE 107–108. Related: P16; C08.
Structure and inputs: Resolve action → update public state and remaining resources → account for opposing response → decide whether to extend or conserve.
Expected effect / use: A local gain can invite counterplay and alter risk.
Original illustration: The exchange was over. She still had to decide what she could afford to leave exposed.
Failure: Narrator declares victory before remaining scoring or effects are settled.
Acceptance check: Refresh state ledger and avoid hindsight certainty.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S09 — Training under variable conditions
Level: L6. Evidence: MUS-WATER 49; MUS-WIND 72–73; YAG-KILLING 83–84,94–98. Related: M64; M100; M102.
Structure and inputs: Isolate decision skill → practice with changing inputs → record errors → progress only after repeatable performance.
Expected effect / use: Learning becomes usable in unfamiliar states.
Original illustration: For the next set, they changed the opening hands instead of replaying the same answer.
Failure: Montage instantly turns novice into unbeatable player.
Acceptance check: Track distinct trials, mistakes, constraints; no claimed improvement without measurements.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S10 — Reset without erasing consequences
Level: L2. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 62–63; YAG-LIFE 107–112. Related: P05; P18; M100.
Structure and inputs: Recognize attention caught by previous error → recheck current facts → select best remaining legal option → review error later.
Expected effect / use: Character regains useful focus while loss of resources persists.
Original illustration: He could review the mistake after the match; the score in front of him still needed an answer.
Failure: Calmness magically reverses an illegal action or loss.
Acceptance check: State and penalties persist; obey judge instructions and round clock.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S11 — Opponent model and revision
Level: L6. Evidence: MUS-FIRE 54,56; MUS-WIND 65–74; YAG-LIFE 99–100. Related: P13; M71; B06.
Structure and inputs: Learn plausible opponent objectives and archetype constraints → separate public facts from assumptions → update beliefs after evidence.
Expected effect / use: Rivals make coherent choices; predictions may fail.
Original illustration: She expected the common list, then noticed evidence that made her widen the possibilities.
Failure: Narrator certifies a rival's hidden hand from a facial expression.
Acceptance check: Document source of evidence and competing hypotheses; avoid stereotyping.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## FRY-S12 — Fallback when preferred means is absent
Level: L3. Evidence: MUS-EARTH 25–28; MUS-FIRE 63; YAG-NOSWORD 113–116. Related: P03; P07; C05.
Structure and inputs: State objective → preferred route unavailable → enumerate remaining legal tools → choose a credible reduced outcome or concede when justified.
Expected effect / use: Resourcefulness without plot armor.
Original illustration: Her usual opening was unavailable, and she settled on a slower plan she could actually execute.
Failure: Inventing a topdeck, card effect or special rule to rescue the protagonist.
Acceptance check: Log the lost option, legal remaining alternatives and cost; failure remains possible.
Status: extracted; no independent test.

## Original block exercises (do not reuse as manuscript text)

FRY-B01 | S01/S09 | Review after a practice win. POV: unnamed player; knowledge limited to own choices and public outcome.
At the end of the session, Ellis picked out the game he'd won quickest. His partner expected him to ask for another round. Instead he turned the score sheet over and asked which decision had given her the most trouble. She pointed to a different part of the game than he had expected.
State change: favorable outcome becomes a testable review question. Failure: partner reveals unrelated private information as fact. Domain: generic review; no replay claimed.

FRY-B02 | S04/S05/S11 | Competing legal plans, conceptual only.
Dana had prepared for the expensive answer. Her opponent did something cheaper, and she had to reconsider what the unused resources might be for. There were several explanations. She could afford to play around one more of them, but doing so would cost her the chance she had been waiting for.
State change: narrower expectation becomes uncertainty with a real opportunity cost. Failure: declaring the hidden answer known. Domain: requires a sourced legal state if instantiated.

FRY-B03 | S07/S10/S12 | Practice setback and new objective.
The first plan had gone wrong early. Luis asked his practice partner to leave the position exactly as it was while he worked through the remaining choices. He eventually found a line worth trying, although he could no longer pursue the original objective on his own terms.
State change: recover decision agency without restoring lost resources. Failure: retroactively changing the board. Domain: practice exercise only, no implied legal tournament rewind.

FRY-B04 | S02/S03/S08 | A narrow success creates a broader decision.
The exchange had gone in Morgan's favor. At the next pause, she counted what she had committed and looked again at the rest of the table. What had seemed like a reason to keep pushing was now an open question. She asked her partner to record that position for their next practice session.
State change: success followed by a resource reassessment. Failure: the opponent stops responding because the plot requires it. Domain: conceptual; exact action windows must be sourced.
