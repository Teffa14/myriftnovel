# MAJ — source-grounded analysis
Pietari Majuri, Narrative in Magic: The Gathering: A Case Study on Invested MTG Players, master's thesis, Tampere University, April 2023. Author-supplied 64-page PDF. Exploratory qualitative study, not a novel or a reader experiment. Physical page references match printed pagination.

## Evidence ledger
- pp. 5–7: researcher describes personal involvement and gap in physical TCG narrative research.
- pp. 8–11: broad concept of narrative encompasses written, visual and player-created stories. Salter and Sullivan's event ordering/content distinction and Martin's demiurge/artificer model are secondary literature, not new survey findings.
- pp. 12–16: stores, differing play contexts and storyworld; gameplay combinations may diverge from canonical stories. Transfer concepts only, not MTG rules.
- pp. 17–21: names, types, rules, art and flavor text contribute differently. Figure 1 identifies components; figures 4–5 contrast immediately intelligible imagery and references demanding lore knowledge. Neither art nor flavor text establishes legal effects.
- pp. 22–25: novels, comics, online stories and fan media extend engagement. Lang's simulation/evocation distinction (p. 23) is cited secondary literature. Narrative flow in play is a useful lens, not evidence of novel reader preferences.
- pp. 26–31: open-ended survey in Finland-based online communities and store social media, open two weeks; English or Finnish, translated Finnish quotations. 46 adults aged 19–44; 43 male, two female, one other; about 13 minutes per response. Thematic analysis. No probability sampling or follow-up interviews. Raw respondent records absent.
- pp. 32–34, §4.2: self-reported attitudes vary from indifference to narrative being essential; some value artwork, worldbuilding and mechanics enriching one another. No measured behavioral effect.
- pp. 35–37, §4.3: some follow stories, fan media and discussion beyond play; some avoid fandom spaces; others learn incidentally. Do not assume all players consume supplemental fiction.
- pp. 38–41, §4.4: some competitive players prioritize performance over thematic deckbuilding yet enjoy incidental humorous or resonant interactions. Majuri proposes context-dependent play types rather than fixed player types.
- pp. 41–43, §4.5: participant criticism of perceived story quality, coherence, distribution and monetization is subjective, not independently established decline or corporate misconduct.
- pp. 43–45, §4.6: nostalgia can attach to earlier play, social experiences, art, printings and pet cards; no universal trait or measured retention effect.
- pp. 45–50, §4.7: favorite cards reflect varying preferences, including mechanical value, art, self-contained stories and external media. Figures 9–10 illustrate atmosphere, figures 12–13 an artist's work, figure 14 a connection with supplemental fiction.
- pp. 51–54: distinguishes worldbuilding/story from narrative emerging in play. Vorthos and Mel, Rosewater's aesthetic lenses discussed by Majuri, need not be mutually exclusive. Figure 15 illustrates mechanical/thematic resonance. Social history matters beyond the cards. Acquisition/retention discussion is interpretive, not causal.
- pp. 55–57: limitations include skewed sample, author positionality, surface responses and ambiguity of narrative terminology. Findings are not representative of Riftbound or novel readers.
- pp. 58–64: references and survey instrument; three background and five open-ended questions in appendix. No independent review of cited literature or raw-data reanalysis.

## Transfer
Distinguish source observations, secondary literature, and editorial extrapolation. Apply to independent fictional motivations, object histories, background talk, contrasting approaches to play and human consequences of verified actions. The 2023 Finnish MTG sample cannot establish actual 2026 US Riftbound behavior or market demand. No MTG rules imported into Riftbound, no fabricated demographics, no canon changes.
