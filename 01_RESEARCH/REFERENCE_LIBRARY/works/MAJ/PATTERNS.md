# MAJ — ten editorial mechanism candidates
Original editorial extrapolations from a qualitative study, not proven causal effects or certified independent techniques. Cross-references identify possible overlap, not novelty. Examples are noncanonical American English, not a phrase bank.

## MAJ-M01 — Context-dependent play motivation
Level: L6. Source: pp. 38–41, 52–53. Related: 48P-M10; B06.
Structure: Context changes → current goal changes → player makes an observable choice; competitive, aesthetic and social motives can coexist.
Original application: A regular optimizes a tournament deck but chooses favorite artwork at home.
Failure/acceptance: Do not freeze people into casual or competitive types; establish the immediate goal and independent motive.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M02 — Attachment versus deck efficiency
Level: L3. Source: pp. 43–46, 50. Related: FRY-S02; 48P-M09.
Structure: Personal attachment → compare tournament goal and alternatives → choose and bear a cost.
Original application: A player keeps a beloved printing in a binder while changing a tournament list.
Failure/acceptance: Separate printing, ownership, sentiment, price and legality; no authorial upgrade of a favorite card.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M03 — Emergent micro-narrative
Level: L2. Source: pp. 16, 20–21, 39–40, 51. Related: FRY-S03; FRY-S11.
Structure: Legal game sequence → unexpected or resonant result → character-specific interpretation → optional later retelling.
Original application: A player remembers an unusual exchange because the table found the situation funny.
Failure/acceptance: Verify state and actions; do not rig draws, rules or opponent choices for a joke.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M04 — Mechanical and aesthetic resonance
Level: L2. Source: pp. 17–20, 46–47, 52–53; figs. 1, 9–10, 15. Related: P24; B03; FRY-S02.
Structure: Verified mechanic → consider presentation → character-specific response → return to the decision.
Original application: One player notices the thematic effect while another cares about the resource exchange.
Failure/acceptance: Art and lore cannot establish rules; verify actual Riftbound card identity and effects separately.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M05 — Object-linked personal memory
Level: L5. Source: pp. 43–45, 48–50, 53–54. Related: T-002; MED-M01.
Structure: Material object → continuity-grounded memory → current choice → changed meaning.
Original application: A worn deck box reminds a player of a friend they rarely see now.
Failure/acceptance: Record object provenance and memory owner; no invented formative event or compulsory sentiment.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M06 — Off-table belonging
Level: L6. Source: pp. 34–37, 49–50, 54. Related: Community protocol; 48P-M02.
Structure: Independent activity → social contact or personal interest → concrete action → optional thread.
Original application: A visitor collects an order and discusses artwork without entering a tournament.
Failure/acceptance: Do not force collectors to compete; activity must make sense without protagonist.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M07 — Knowledge depth and access
Level: L3. Source: pp. 20–21, 35–37, 55–56; figs. 4–5. Related: P24; B03.
Structure: Observable problem → necessary context in ordinary language → optional deeper reference → comprehensible decision.
Original application: A newcomer understands why a trade matters without recognizing the illustrated character.
Failure/acceptance: No lore lecture required for comprehension; preserve exact technical terms when necessary.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M08 — Changing relationship with the hobby
Level: L5. Source: pp. 38–41, 43–45, 51, 54. Related: MED-M10; 48P-M12.
Structure: Established motive → changed circumstances → revised activity → consequences.
Original application: A former tournament regular attends occasional casual nights because work changed.
Failure/acceptance: No universal career trajectory; track established goals and dated commitments.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M09 — Community disagreement about quality
Level: L3. Source: pp. 41–45, 55–56. Related: 48P-M04; 48P-M11.
Structure: Specific complaint → differing viewpoint → evidence or uncertainty → practical response or open disagreement.
Original application: Two customers disagree about newer art while making unrelated purchases.
Failure/acceptance: Attribute subjective opinions; no claimed consensus or unsupported publisher misconduct.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## MAJ-M10 — Causal play and narrative integrity
Level: L4. Source: pp. 22–23, 40–42, 51. Related: B02; FRY-S01; MED-M12.
Structure: Initial stakes and state → meaningful legal decision → opponent response → outcome → human consequence.
Original application: A tactical reversal changes a conversation after the match, even if the player loses.
Failure/acceptance: No predetermined win or missing causal links; verify scoped replay and preserve opponent agency.
Status: extracted; no reader or gameplay test.

## Original exercises

### MAJ-B01 — Different goals, same person
Candidates: MAJ-M01, MAJ-M02.
At the tournament table, Tess had changed the list after three poor testing sessions. The following evening she spent ten minutes comparing two printings for the binder she kept at home. Her friend asked which was stronger. Tess turned the binder toward her. "This one's the one I wanted."
State change and boundary: A performance-oriented choice coexists with aesthetic preference. No actual card effect or superiority asserted.

### MAJ-B02 — A reason to be there
Candidates: MAJ-M05, MAJ-M06.
Milo had come in to collect an order. He stopped beside the display because an illustration reminded him of a poster from his old apartment. The employee asked whether he'd signed up for the event. He shook his head and went back to checking the condition of the card.
State change and boundary: Independent noncompetitive activity. Fictional memory, no new canon.

### MAJ-B03 — Remembering an exchange
Candidates: MAJ-M03, MAJ-M04.
After the practice game, Rosa and Dan disagreed about which exchange had been most interesting. Dan had written down the resources that were spent. Rosa remembered the table's reaction when the situation reversed. They agreed to review the recorded position before the next session.
State change and boundary: Different interpretations of one event; actual game state remains separate and must be verified if depicted.

### MAJ-B04 — Different knowledge, same conversation
Candidates: MAJ-M07, MAJ-M09.
One customer mentioned an old character design while another was looking for a replacement sleeve. A third asked what they were talking about, and the first showed him the card and explained the comparison. The conversation returned to the sleeves.
State change and boundary: Accessible context without exposition overload. No specific lore claim.

### MAJ-B05 — Participation changes
Candidates: MAJ-M08, MAJ-M10.
Jules used to plan his week around the shop event. Lately he'd been stopping in after work, playing a short practice session when someone was free. He still wanted to improve, but this month he had declined two invitations that would have kept him out too late.
State change and boundary: Changed schedule alters play mode; no specific rules, actual outcome or character canon claimed.
