# MED — psychological and ethical narrative candidates
Twelve grouped candidates; overlap is explicit rather than counted as novel independent techniques. Status extracted, not reader-tested. Examples use original English expression and are noncanonical; never recycle them in manuscript prose. Source anchors refer to the edition-specific paragraph ordinals defined in ANALYSIS.md.

## MED-M01 — Values learned through relationships
Level: L6. Sources: I ¶¶1–17; VI ¶30; XI ¶44. Related: 48P-M01; 48P-M05; B06.
Structure: Concrete influence → identify learned conduct → independently apply or revise it → acknowledge contributor.
Narrative function: Mentorship has a history and the learner retains agency.
Original sentence: She remembered who had taught her to check the result before defending the method.
Failure: Mentor delivers a doctrine that instantly changes a personality.
Acceptance check: Connect an existing relationship and an observable changed choice; no invented past.

## MED-M02 — Event, interpretation, response
Level: L2. Sources: II ¶15; III ¶11; IV ¶8,12; VIII ¶49; XII ¶18–20. Related: 48P-M03; 48P-M11; FRY-S10.
Structure: Record event → distinguish impression from verified fact → examine alternative explanations → choose proportionate action.
Narrative function: Separates a setback from a character's initial story about it.
Original sentence: He knew the message had gone unanswered; he didn't know why.
Failure: A character treats an injury, debt or misconduct as unreal because interpretation matters.
Acceptance check: Retain objective loss, emotion and uncertainty; no clinical claims.

## MED-M03 — Bounded control and present decision
Level: L3. Sources: II ¶5–8; VI ¶41; VII ¶8,29; VIII ¶23; XII ¶3–4,35. Related: FRY-S10; FRY-S12; 48P-M09.
Structure: Name goal and constraint → distinguish controllable decision from unavailable outcome → take next feasible action → track consequence.
Narrative function: Enables recovery without magical certainty.
Original sentence: The result was already on the sheet. His next choice was how to spend the remaining practice time.
Failure: Anxiety disappears permanently or acceptance becomes passivity.
Acceptance check: Respect resources, clock, rules and real consequences; retain unresolved emotion.

## MED-M04 — Correction without identity collapse
Level: L3. Sources: IV ¶13; V ¶9; VI ¶21; VIII ¶16; X ¶33. Related: FRY-S01; FRY-S09; 48P-M05.
Structure: Claim → counterevidence → acknowledge limited error → amend procedure → retest.
Narrative function: Creates believable intellectual growth without humiliation or omniscience.
Original sentence: She crossed out the conclusion but kept the data.
Failure: Character concedes everything to a mentor or treats every criticism as valid.
Acceptance check: Identify specific evidence, revised belief and new test; no guaranteed result.

## MED-M05 — Sustainable discipline and ordinary duty
Level: L6. Sources: III ¶5,12; V ¶1,9; VIII ¶12; XII ¶8. Related: FRY-S09; 48P-M09.
Structure: Recurring duty → reluctance or fatigue → bounded commitment → rest or repair → repeated action.
Narrative function: Progress becomes visible through routines and interruptions.
Original sentence: He shortened the session so he could make his shift the next morning.
Failure: Sleep deprivation and burnout are equated with virtue.
Acceptance check: Maintain work, sleep, budget and consent boundaries; no health efficacy claim.

## MED-M06 — Independent standards versus applause
Level: L5. Sources: III ¶6–7; IV ¶19–21,34–38; VI ¶50,58; VII ¶47; VIII ¶1,22,46. Related: 48P-M04; 48P-M10; FRY-S01.
Structure: Public appraisal → compare with personal responsibility and evidence → choose conduct → accept uncertain reception.
Narrative function: Allows ambition and ethical restraint to coexist.
Original sentence: The interview went well; the error in her preparation still needed attention.
Failure: A player dismisses all feedback or becomes indifferent to meaningful career goals.
Acceptance check: Separate public metrics, personal goals and ethical evaluation.

## MED-M07 — Proportionate response to offense
Level: L3. Sources: II ¶1; VI ¶6,49; VII ¶22,26; IX ¶11,20,39; XI ¶23–33. Related: 48P-M08; 48P-M11.
Structure: Offense → establish facts and responsibility → notice own impulse → decide boundary, correction or disengagement → retain consequences.
Narrative function: Produces conflict without treating retaliation as the only credible response.
Original sentence: She objected to the remark and returned to the scheduling problem.
Failure: Forgiveness requires tolerating abuse, suppressing emotion or avoiding reports.
Acceptance check: Preserve safety, evidence, agency, proportionate enforcement and right to leave.

## MED-M08 — Reciprocal support without ownership
Level: L6. Sources: I ¶¶7,13–17; V ¶6,38; VII ¶7,69–70; IX ¶1,23. Related: 48P-M02; 48P-M07; B06.
Structure: Identify need and capacity → offer or accept bounded help → negotiate responsibility → acknowledge without demanding indebtedness.
Narrative function: Develops authentic teamwork and independence.
Original sentence: He accepted the ride after confirming when his teammate needed to leave.
Failure: Helping someone creates entitlement to their loyalty or affection.
Acceptance check: Explicit consent, terms and ability to decline; no emotional debt.

## MED-M09 — Commitments over convenience
Level: L3. Sources: III ¶7; VI ¶49; IX ¶1,4,16,23; XII ¶1,19,22. Related: 48P-M07; 48P-M09; P19.
Structure: Opportunity → identify promise or rule → evaluate conflict → disclose or decline → record cost.
Narrative function: Makes ethical agency materially consequential.
Original sentence: She turned down the extra session rather than pretend her earlier commitment had vanished.
Failure: Principled behavior is always rewarded or all competing duties have a simple answer.
Acceptance check: Specify obligations and real alternatives; do not invent canon or contracts.

## MED-M10 — Perspective without erasing stakes
Level: L2. Sources: II ¶11–17; IV ¶34–38,45; VII ¶6,18,34; IX ¶36; XII ¶23,34–38. Related: FRY-S10; 48P-M04.
Structure: Immediate setback → place in longer horizon → distinguish temporary status from material loss → select meaningful follow-up.
Narrative function: Reduces melodrama while keeping emotional and financial stakes real.
Original sentence: A week later the ranking still bothered him, but it no longer decided what he would practice.
Failure: Grief, pain, rent or a lost opportunity is dismissed as trivial.
Acceptance check: Track actual loss and allow distress; no requirement to accept preventable injustice.

## MED-M11 — Adaptation with an ethical boundary
Level: L3. Sources: IV ¶1,13,38,44; V ¶20; VII ¶18; X ¶12; XII ¶8,12. Related: FRY-S07; FRY-S12; 48P-M12.
Structure: Obstruction → inspect available resources → redesign feasible action → preserve prior commitment or renegotiate openly.
Narrative function: Obstacle changes the method, not necessarily the purpose.
Original sentence: The venue change ruined their schedule, so they agreed on a smaller test.
Failure: An obstacle automatically creates an advantage or excuses rule violations.
Acceptance check: Verify feasibility, obligations, alternative cost and genuine possibility of failure.

## MED-M12 — Conduct as the evidence of character
Level: L5. Sources: III ¶5–7; VI ¶30; IX ¶16,23; X ¶18,35; XI ¶20,44; XII ¶19,22. Related: 48P-M05; 48P-M10; P20.
Structure: Stated value → situational pressure → observable choice → effect on others → repeated or corrected behavior.
Narrative function: Character development is demonstrated longitudinally instead of announced.
Original sentence: The next time the mistake appeared, he reported it before anyone asked.
Failure: One virtuous action proves permanent transformation or all motives are disclosed.
Acceptance check: Use consistent POV and continuity; permit relapse and consequences.

## Original blocks
### MED-B01 — After a setback
Candidates: MED-M02, MED-M03. Noncanonical.
The score was already recorded when Erin opened her notebook. She started to write what her opponent must have been thinking, stopped, and replaced it with the decisions she could actually remember. One position would need another look. The rest could wait until she got home.
State change: Initial assumption becomes a bounded review task; loss remains recorded.
POV boundary: Only own recollection and public outcome known; no card interaction asserted.

### MED-B02 — Public correction
Candidates: MED-M04, MED-M06. Noncanonical.
Cal had explained the same conclusion twice before a teammate pointed out that one of the recorded trials used a different starting condition. He checked the sheet. At the next meeting he withdrew that conclusion and asked whether they could repeat the trial properly.
State change: Specific belief revised under scrutiny; reputation effect unknown.
POV boundary: Evidence is the trial sheet, not a claim about teammate's motive.

### MED-B03 — Work and practice
Candidates: MED-M05, MED-M09. Noncanonical.
The group offered another evening of testing. Talia checked her work rota and the time she'd need to get home. She could join for the first hour, she said, and asked them to send her the remaining questions rather than promise the whole night.
State change: A bounded agreement replaces an unsustainable commitment.
POV boundary: No payment, schedule detail or private character canon invented.

### MED-B04 — A disagreement with a boundary
Candidates: MED-M07, MED-M12. Noncanonical.
When the organizer asked why Lee was upset, he described the registration decision instead of repeating the insult he'd heard afterward. He wanted the procedure reviewed. He was willing to discuss it again, provided the next conversation stayed on the issue.
State change: Anger becomes a concrete request with a boundary.
POV boundary: No claim that the organizer acted intentionally; complaint remains open.

### MED-B05 — Receiving help
Candidates: MED-M01, MED-M08. Noncanonical.
Nora had been trying to solve the preparation problem alone. A friend offered to review her notes, and she agreed to share the parts she had permission to circulate. By the end of the evening they had a shorter set of questions, not a completed solution.
State change: Consensual support changes the work while retaining ownership.
POV boundary: No confidential deck information, forced dependence or validated tactical result.

### MED-B06 — A changed objective
Candidates: MED-M10, MED-M11. Noncanonical.
The canceled event left Malik with an unused day off and a nonrefundable expense. He couldn't recover the money. After talking with his practice partner, he decided to spend part of the afternoon testing the problem they'd been postponing, then go home.
State change: Loss persists; available time is redirected to a useful goal.
POV boundary: The replacement activity has no guaranteed benefit; no exact amount asserted.
