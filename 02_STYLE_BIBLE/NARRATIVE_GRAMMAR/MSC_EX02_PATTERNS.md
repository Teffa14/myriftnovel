# Gramática MSC-EX02 — explicar un TCG mediante experiencia
10 patrones candidatos (M09–M18), cuatro bloques originales (MB04–MB07). Fuente cualitativa: ../../01_RESEARCH/REFERENCE_LIBRARY/works/MSC/TUTORIAL_EXCERPT_ANALYSIS.md. Los ejemplos ingleses son ejercicios independientes y no canon. Las cartas, estados y procedimientos son hipotéticos hasta validación reglamentaria.

## M09 — Objeto familiar como puente
Estructura: elemento nuevo → referencia conocida → diferencia que importa.
Example: Nora recognized the tournament software from the local events. This time, however, each pairing carried a table number she had to find in a much larger room.
Control: la comparación aclara una diferencia real.

## M10 — Función antes de terminología
Estructura: problema → herramienta → nombre técnico.
Example: Adrian wanted to see whether the deck could recover after a weak opening. Nora set aside a separate sheet for games that began that way. They were testing consistency, rather than the matchup alone.
Control: nombrar el concepto después de mostrar su utilidad, sin falsear definiciones.

## M11 — Regla seguida de caso concreto
Estructura: principio → ejemplo observable → consecuencia.
Example: A deck could perform well in practice and still exceed his budget. Adrian priced the missing cards before committing to the list. Two of them accounted for most of the difference.
Control: cada cifra o regla debe modificar una elección.

## M12 — Analogía con conocimiento previo
Estructura: concepto desconocido → esquema familiar → límite de la comparación.
Example: He had learned to save time on difficult exam questions. Testing a deck required a similar discipline, although here the decisions he skipped were often the ones he needed to study.
Control: precisar dónde deja de funcionar la analogía.

## M13 — Descripción guiada por uso
Estructura: detalle físico → acción → función.
Example: Nora slid the empty sleeve across the table. It marked the card they wanted to replace, so they could discuss alternatives without losing track of the original list.
Control: los objetos sirven a la acción, no al inventario.

## M14 — Reducir datos a rol estratégico
Estructura: información técnica → clasificación funcional → necesidad del plan.
Example: The first candidate gave the deck another way to survive an early threat. The second offered a payoff for surviving it. Adrian needed to determine which problem occurred more often.
Control: las funciones requieren evidencia de efectos y testing; no declarar superioridad absoluta.

## M15 — Aritmética de opciones
Estructura: recurso disponible → costes → alternativas asequibles → coste de oportunidad.
Example: He had enough money for either the replacement cards or a return ticket to the qualifier. Buying both would leave him short of the entry fee.
Control: verificar aritmética, presupuesto y costes omitidos.

## M16 — Conocimiento demostrado por acción
Estructura: nueva regla → inferencia → elección observable.
Example: After reading the event requirements, Adrian removed the practice list from his registration folder and uploaded the version he had actually tested.
Control: verificar procedimiento y legalidad del evento.

## M17 — Expectativa comprobable
Estructura: hipótesis técnica → prueba prevista → resultado aún abierto.
Example: The cheaper list looked consistent on paper. Nora proposed a series of games against the opening that had caused them the most trouble.
Control: el resultado debe proceder de pruebas, no del narrador.

## M18 — Cierre en umbral de aplicación
Estructura: aprendizaje suficiente → acción iniciada → resultado pendiente legítimo.
Example: Adrian finished reviewing the matchup notes and placed his deck on the table. His opponent offered a cut.
Control: alternar con capítulos que sí resuelven el conflicto; no cortar antes de cada resultado.

## MB04 — Enseñar una diferencia competitiva con consecuencias
Nora divided the results into two columns.

“Four wins,” Adrian said.

“Four games where you opened well.”

He checked the sheet. In the other six, the deck had rarely recovered. The overall result had told him how often he won; the second column suggested when he was in trouble.

“What do we change?”

“First, we find out whether it's the opening or what you do with it.”

They set up another game.

Function: dato → distinción → hipótesis → prueba. No asumir causalidad por una muestra pequeña.

## MB05 — Explicación de cartas orientada a roles
Adrian had brought three possible replacements for the same slot.

Nora asked him to describe what he wanted from each one before they looked at prices.

The first was intended to help him recover from an early disadvantage. The second supported a more aggressive plan. The third was useful in fewer situations, but those situations had cost them several games the week before.

Adrian moved the third card closer to the notebook.

“Let's test the narrow one first.”

Function: función de cada alternativa → comparación con problema observado → elección experimental. No inventar efectos ni afirmar que el tercer candidato gana.

## MB06 — Economía como restricción dramática
The cards arrived in a small envelope. Adrian entered the cost in his spreadsheet before opening it.

He had enough left for the entry fee, but not for the train. That was why Nora had asked for his travel dates before recommending the changes.

He opened the group chat.

“Anyone driving on Saturday?”

There was no answer yet.

Function: objeto → coste → comprensión de una preocupación ajena → decisión. La aritmética crea una necesidad social.

## MB07 — De observación a ejecución
Nora paused the replay on the turn Adrian had marked.

He had written that the deck needed a different answer. On the recording, he noticed how early he had committed the resources that might have been useful later.

“Same list?” she asked.

“Same list.”

He wanted to test whether his decision, rather than the deck itself, was the problem.

They started another game.

Function: evidencia → revisión de explicación → acción que pone a prueba hipótesis. Cualquier jugada detallada requiere replay válido.
