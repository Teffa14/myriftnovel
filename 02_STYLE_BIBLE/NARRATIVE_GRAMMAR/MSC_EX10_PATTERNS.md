# MSC-EX10 — organizaciones, información y memoria competitiva
Diez entradas M81–M90 y cuatro bloques MB32–MB35. Fuente: ../../01_RESEARCH/REFERENCE_LIBRARY/works/MSC/INSTITUTIONAL_META_EXCERPT_ANALYSIS.md. Ejercicios originales no canónicos. Limitar investigación competitiva a fuentes públicas y autorizadas.

## M81 — Escasez como presión informativa
Inventario conocido → unidades localizadas → incertidumbre restante.
Example: Three opponents had published their lists. The fourth had not, and Nora could only prepare against what she knew.
Control: ausencia de información no demuestra amenaza.

## M82 — Conducta visible de intención ambigua
Acción pública → interpretaciones alternativas → decisión prudente.
Example: Two teams had registered for a small practice event. Marcus could not tell whether they wanted results or simply more games.
Control: no atribuir intención privada como hecho.

## M83 — Rivalidad mediante diálogo con subtexto
Encuentro → pregunta superficial → respuesta limitada → tensión preservada.
Example: “Playing the same list this weekend?” Leah asked. “We'll see,” Marcus said, putting his deck away.
Control: mantener agencia y evitar antagonismo permanente.

## M84 — Precedente histórico con efecto actual
Evento pasado → cambio de práctica → decisión presente.
Example: After their previous season ended with little preparation for an unfamiliar matchup, the team began setting aside one night each month for unusual lists.
Control: comprobar historia, no insertar enciclopedia desconectada.

## M85 — Inteligencia competitiva legal
Pregunta estratégica → evidencia pública → hipótesis → prueba.
Example: Nora compared the published results with the available decklists. She wanted to know whether the apparent change came from adoption or performance.
Control: respetar privacidad, reglas y límites de muestra.

## M86 — Señal imperfecta y coste de búsqueda
Objetivo específico → consulta demasiado amplia → información irrelevante → ajuste.
Example: Adrian asked for advice about his deck and received twelve suggestions for a matchup he was not preparing to play.
Control: no tratar ruido como evidencia.

## M87 — Desencuentro causal entre subtramas
A espera conducta de B → B cambia por motivo propio → oportunidad se desplaza.
Example: Leah reserved a practice slot for Adrian. He had already arranged to work that evening after seeing the travel bill.
Control: ambas decisiones deben tener causas previas.

## M88 — Experiencia biográfica como recurso
Recuerdo pertinente → asociación nueva → acción actual.
Example: Adrian remembered how he had organized drills at his old club. He adapted the schedule for their shorter practice sessions.
Control: evitar recuerdo como solución milagrosa.

## M89 — Simplificación de un prototipo
Intento complejo → identificar variable central → diseño reducido → prueba.
Example: Nora restored the original list and kept only one of the changes. Now they could test the question that had started the discussion.
Control: controlar condiciones y versionar listas.

## M90 — Validación parcial y cierre
Hito técnico cumplido → siguiente dato desconocido → expectativa delimitada.
Example: The list passed registration checks. Adrian still had no results against the matchup he feared.
Control: legalidad no equivale a calidad competitiva.

## MB32 — El meta conserva memoria
Nora found the old matchup sheet in the shared folder.

“Why do we still test this list?”

Leah showed her the notes from the previous season. The team had spent weeks preparing for the most popular decks and barely any time on the one that had eliminated two of its players.

“We don't need to spend all night on it,” Leah said. “But I want at least a few games before we call it irrelevant.”

Function: historia institucional → política actual → límite de recursos. No asumir resultados reales.

## MB33 — Interpretar señales públicas
Marcus noticed that two players had changed their published lists since the last event.

He sent the links to Nora.

“Same reason, you think?”

She compared the results they had made public. One had lost twice in the matchup they were studying. The other had not played it at all.

“We don't know yet.”

She added the changes to the next testing brief without assigning either player a motive.

Function: observación → hipótesis múltiple → prueba legal; información pública exclusivamente.

## MB34 — Dos objetivos que no coinciden
Leah wanted to discuss the variant Adrian had tested on Tuesday. She sent him a message asking for the list.

* * *

Adrian had spent his morning preparing for a different matchup. The Tuesday version was still saved, but he had moved on after the last session.

He saw Leah's message during his break and sent her the file.

“What did you find?”

She had not played a game with it yet. She sent him the question she wanted to test.

Function: objetivos independientes, comunicación y descubrimiento pendiente. Mantener límites de conocimiento.

## MB35 — Validación por etapas
Adrian removed two changes from the draft. The remaining one addressed the problem he had written down at the start of the evening.

Nora checked the list against the event requirements. It was legal.

“Ready?”

“For practice,” Adrian said.

He opened the first recording and prepared the scenario they wanted to reproduce.

Function: simplificación → validación legal → prueba competitiva pendiente. No confundir hitos.
