## Problem diagnosed
The previous production grammar is useful as a private planning and verification tool. Its repeated observation–inference–decision–consequence structure can flatten everyday experience into problem solving. A chapter can satisfy the technical checklist and still sound interchangeable with another contemporary serial. The goal is to make the narrator's attention, rhythm, omissions, interpersonal friction and scene shape arise from this specific cast.

## The literary identity
Contemporary American English, grounded working-class and competitive TCG fiction. Close third-person past; a narrator whose selection of detail changes with the focal character. Ordinary intimacy and imperfect friendship, competitive precision only when the decision calls for it, social humor grounded in people who have known one another long enough to be awkward or unguarded, money and time experienced rather than constantly accounted for. Emotional restraint does not mean emotional uniformity. Anger, desire, envy, self-deception, relief, affection and grief can shape the grammar. Never import another author's distinctive style or manufacture a regional accent from ethnicity.

## Character-specific attention (proposed voice profiles, not new biography)
Nico: notices relative costs, commitments and opportunity; during play he can be absorbed in the position, during life he may overlook what matters to another person. Let his confidence, fatigue and embarrassment alter his thoughts; avoid narrating every inference or turning him into a spreadsheet.
Eli: physical competence, practical improvisation, playful curiosity and people; attention can wander toward an interesting thing even when it is irrelevant to a match. His generosity emerges through voluntary action; his own worries and ambitions have equal narrative legitimacy. Do not impose a permanent joke cadence.
June: composition, fit, comparison, visual choices and standards; social observation and dry humor can differ from her precise tournament speech. Permit emotional imprecision and attraction without explaining them as a tactical decision. Do not assume behavior from her cultural identity.
Maya: notices who is waiting, what a space feels like, what people expect and where ordinary logistics fail. Her professional confidence can coexist with impatience, pleasure, insecurity and a desire for her own life. Avoid making every exchange a service interaction.
Lucia: observes the demands people place on her, shared household habits and her own peer world. Give her language and attentional priorities distinct from Nico, with concrete academic and social experiences as established. Avoid making her a moral commentator.
Marcus: notices craft, prior experience, trade-offs and his own limitations. He may be mistaken, tired, ordinary or playful; do not make each appearance a lesson, parable or Socratic exercise.
These profiles are flexible lenses, never obligatory verbal ticks. A line must fit the specific speaker, relationship, motive and situation. In a private blind voice review, mask names and attribution from selected comparable passages and assess whether differences are supported by language and attention, not solely by the activity or subject. Report the actual reviewer and limitations; this is editorial evidence, not an authorship test.

## Production method
LPM-A — Private scene reality. Record verified circumstances, what each participant is occupied with, their independent wants, what they know, material constraints, and technical scope. Keep this machinery off the page. Choose the lived event, not a stock prose template.
LPM-B — Selective viewpoint. Establish what this focal character attends to, fails to notice, assumes, misremembers or cannot express at this moment. Details should earn their place through attention or atmosphere. Do not insert decorative sensory inventory. Use physical work and movement where the situation supports them; avoid default looking, phone-checking, nodding, smiling and turning as dialogue spacers.
LPM-C — Scene-specific form. Choose among sustained action, interrupted conversation, ambient social scene, compressed passage of time, interior reverie, technical contest, ordinary shared activity, asymmetric confrontation, or quiet observation. These are options, not a rotation quota. Use mixed forms organically; scenes can overlap purposes and end before every question is resolved. A quiet chapter may be enjoyable without a revelation or lesson. Keep enough reader orientation and causal continuity.
LPM-D — First draft by experience. Write fresh prose from the event and character rather than translating beat bullets one by one. Allow dialogue to begin mid-topic, leave space for silence, permit ordinary replies, interruptions and independent conversations. Do not force subtext, wit, a callback or conflict into every line. Let a paragraph carry movement, thought, speech and sensory perception together when that is natural. Break paragraphs for actual shifts in speaker, action, attention or emphasis. Preserve intentional short paragraphs, especially in real dialogue.
LPM-E — Structural reread. Read continuously as a reader, without the brief or checklist in view. Locate recurring mini-scenes (observation, explanation, corrected decision), redundant narration after dialogue, uniform action tags, unnecessary clock anchoring, identical scene openings and closes, characters who agree or articulate emotions too neatly, and over-explained themes. Fix the underlying narrative choice first. Do not simply replace flagged words, lengthen paragraphs mechanically, inject errors, random slang or deliberate awkwardness to evade detection.
LPM-F — Distinctness pass. For the current chapter and a rolling set of recent units compare openings, ending modes, repeated syntactic shapes, focal attention, dialogue movement and scene allocations. Run voice_audit.py on exact candidate and relevant archive. Compare like-for-like scene types and investigate material differences in context. Counts trigger inspection, not automatic edits. Review character voice with masked comparable samples where useful. Keep private passage evidence and version history. Preserve deliberate character mannerisms and recurring narrative motifs using NDM's existing documented exceptions; do not retire ordinary speech or a relationship's recognizable ritual merely because it recurs. Do not reuse retired distinctive phrases.
LPM-G — Final protected-state review. Check that the revised text retains facts, legal game actions, POV, chronology, relationships, author directives and independent agency. Re-run affected mechanical and originality checks on exact candidate bytes. Identify remaining weaknesses and actual evidence. Deliver a candidate, not approval. No new chapter before established closeout gates.

## Emotional method
Use AD-0010/0012 with AD-0008. Give the reader time to inhabit a relationship without announcing its eventual payoff. Preserve mundane joy and unresolved desires. A dense scene can use sustained action, incomplete speech, altered perception, or an ordinary continuation after catastrophe; do not require a symbolic final sentence, aphorism or explanation of why it hurts. The last line must be chosen for that scene rather than repeated as a short reflective moral. Distinguish actual narrative impact from predicted or scripted reader responses.

## Division of responsibilities
The Last Draw Studio owns live canon, archive, private planning, game evidence, and exact version control. Author Studio supplies developmental and prose review. Human Writing examines narrative choices and natural language. Avoid AI Writing flags patterns for contextual review; if its deterministic detector was not actually executed, label findings model-only. Writing Assistant may provide focused feedback or craft alternatives, not the governing voice or external humanizer workflow. Writing Style is optional evidence retrieval for authentic relevant author-written samples; a list of titles or AI-assisted prior outputs is not an independent author voice sample. Report actual tools used and results, never imply independent reviewers or external services ran when they did not. Do not stack six generic rewrites over the same passage.

## Acceptance and feedback
Before drafting, identify LPM scene mode and focal attention privately; after drafting, provide actual diagnostic evidence and concrete editorial decisions, compare to the approved corpus, and record one reasoned alternative for a weak major scene when needed. No fixed paragraph length, word blacklist, compulsory sentence fragments or detector score threshold. An author-selected excerpt or alternative becomes a positive voice anchor only after explicit acceptance. Distill approved anchors into abstract decisions, not reusable prose; keep excerpts private. Ask voluntary readers neutral questions about whether voices are distinguishable, what they remember, and where prose feels repetitive, artificial or emotionally flat. Preserve real feedback without inventing responses. Track progress through chapter reviews and arc close; no automatic literary certification. LPM 1.0 is a tested diagnostic foundation and proposed writing workflow, not proof that future prose has improved. The next unapproved candidate is the first application point.
