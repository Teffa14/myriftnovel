# Premise and narrative contract
Nicolas "Nico" Vega, 23, a Los Angeles night-shift logistics employee and resourceful amateur card player, has a knack for analyzing patterns after the fact. He lacks tournament repetitions, money, and a stable team. His main weakness is confusing a good explanation with a good decision and treating help as debt. After an early loss he earns a trial invitation because he notices and honestly admits a mistake. The invitation is a beginning, not instant endorsement.
 
External desire: become a player whose entry fees and travel no longer depend on borrowing and earn an opportunity at the top level. Inner need: accept uncertainty, trust others and define worth independently of results. False belief: if he can anticipate every variable, he will never disappoint anyone again. Fear: losing his independence through reliance on others. Emotional engine: success can improve his career while harming family commitments, financial stability and the team; a loss can produce an insight or strengthen a relationship.
 
Core question: can he become a great player without turning everyone around him into pieces of a strategy? Theme: preparation improves decisions, but cannot remove uncertainty. Secondary themes: access, friendship, fair competition, professional boundaries, and value of work outside the game. Tone: sharp situational humor, grounded drama, earned victories, emotional restraint; never celebrate financial ruin or abusive leadership. 
Five recurring loops: identify matchup → practice → fail/update → compete → aftermath; need → negotiate → cost → payback; relationship tension → decision → consequence → change; public reputation → private uncertainty → corrective action; game update → adaptation → changed opportunities. A chapter must change at least one state, but not all. No forced cliffhanger, repeated adulation, effortless prodigy, financial miracles or cheating rewarded as skill.
