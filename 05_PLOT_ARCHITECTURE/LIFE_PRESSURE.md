# Life pressure — material and emotional consequences (AD-0003)

Author direction, 2026-11-26. Apply to all future planning, chapter briefs and editorial review. The story's appeal depends on the reader experiencing the protagonist's ordinary life and why competitive success matters. Work, rent, scarcity, family, education, social life and personal ambition must operate as actual causes of decisions and losses, rather than decorative mentions. No revision of approved chapters is authorized by this document. All scenario options are noncanonical until drafted, validated and approved.

## The pressure systems

### Work and authority (LP01–LP06)
- LP01: A supervisor has a concrete operational demand (volume, coverage, accuracy or deadline); the employee has an incompatible commitment. Show tasks, hierarchy and response instead of merely reporting the schedule.
- LP02: A request for a shift exchange or time off is refused, conditional or granted at a cost. The character must choose; the affected coworker has independent needs.
- LP03: Physical exertion, disrupted sleep or stress reduces preparation time and decision quality. Establish cause and observable impairment; avoid medical claims or unsafe work as heroism.
- LP04: A genuine mistake leads to correction, embarrassment, lost responsibility or a documented warning, proportionate to the employer's established policy. No arbitrary punishment or invented law.
- LP05: An offer of additional paid hours competes with an important opportunity. Show what the income would buy and what the absence would cost.
- LP06: A good employee discovers that competence does not automatically grant flexibility, recognition or advancement. Preserve a plausible supervisor motive; do not make every authority figure a villain.

### Cash flow, rent and necessities (LP07–LP12)
- LP07: Reconcile actual paycheck, rent allocation, bills, food, transport and cash available before a purchase. The arithmetic must change an option or relationship, not become an accounting lecture.
- LP08: A necessary replacement or unexpected household expense consumes discretionary money. Establish responsibility and whether reimbursement, repair or deferral is realistic.
- LP09: A desired card or event exceeds affordable cash; buying it would violate the established budget boundary. Declining, trading or borrowing by consent produces an observable strategic or social trade-off.
- LP10: An earned payment arrives late relative to a genuine due date. Distinguish promise, cleared cash, withholding, ownership, and debt; do not invent missed payments retroactively.
- LP11: A socially ordinary expense (meal, outing, gift, transport) becomes a dignity or relationship problem when the character cannot afford it. Allow honest refusal, imperfect concealment and practical alternatives.
- LP12: An apparently modest competitive win matters because it pays for a previously established need; a larger future milestone follows AD-0001 and is never cashed out prematurely.

### Household, education and unequal choices (LP13–LP18)
- LP13: Two household members have legitimate incompatible commitments; a choice redistributes time or expense instead of resolving everything through one person accommodating the other.
- LP14: A maintenance or domestic obligation has an inconvenient window and a real downstream consequence; vary its execution rather than repeating the CH9 repair.
- LP15: Returning to education, a certification, or a career opportunity requires money, time and a deadline. The character has a future outside the game.
- LP16: Someone refuses a favor or requests repayment of a previously agreed obligation. Respect their autonomy; no guilt manufactured by convenient villainy.
- LP17: A character compares their material circumstances with better-resourced competitors; show a specific difference in access, rehearsal, travel, or equipment instead of blanket resentment.
- LP18: A sacrifice for someone close is chosen rather than automatically demanded. Let the beneficiary retain goals, consent, and a life of their own. Preserve AD-0001.

### Ensemble, dignity and emotional consequences (LP19–LP24)
- LP19: June's freelance client changes scope, deadline or payment expectations; she protects either her professional standing or competitive preparation with a visible trade-off.
- LP20: Eli's apprenticeship exposes a skill gap, a customer conflict or a wage/time constraint; his humor is a coping method, not proof that nothing hurts.
- LP21: Maya's store has operating costs, limited staffing and dissatisfied customers; a good idea costs labor or revenue and may fail. Owen retains a legitimate business objective.
- LP22: Lucia's coursework, café shifts and her own plans collide; she makes a consequential decision independently of Nico. Do not make her a permanent caretaker or obstacle.
- LP23: Marcus's technical-support work and family responsibilities limit mentorship; he can disappoint, refuse or need help without losing dignity.
- LP24: A private worry affects friendship, intimacy, or social belonging; the character reveals, conceals or addresses it for a specific reason. Other characters respond according to what they actually know.

## Establish the employment world

Introduce recurring fictional workplace characters only as needed: direct supervisor, coworkers, scheduling/payroll contact where appropriate. Define their actual duties, incentives, authority, and limits privately; no names or identities are fixed here. Establish the protagonist's responsibilities, observed task flow, actual scheduled and worked time, commute and recovery. Separate common fictional logistics work from asserted real employment policy. Show ordinary competent work, unpleasant shifts, interpersonal friction and occasional relief. Avoid perpetual abuse and poverty spectacle. A real consequence may persist across multiple chapters.

These are historical opening facts, not an automatically current balance. Reconcile the latest private ledger and elapsed dates; unknown pay dates, deductions, lease terms, bill amounts and current balances stay unknown until established. Illustrative arithmetic: four fewer paid hours at the base rate means $84 less gross, conditional on those hours actually being lost; no assertion of overtime entitlement or net pay. Track due date, actual receipt, obligation owner, cash paid, balance, noncash costs, hours and future commitments. Never fund cards from rent money contrary to canon. Use fictional institutions and verified period facts when specific laws, real costs, transport or employment policies are depicted.

## Per-chapter and rolling-arc control

Each private brief identifies active pressure (LP ID or justified N/A), its owner and independent goal, current state, plausible competing options, consequence and which later opportunity changes. Log actual changes to money, work, energy, relationship, access or plans. A mere mention of money/work without changed options does not count as a fulfilled pressure beat. In rolling three-main-chapter reviews, check whether ordinary-life pressures have been experienced on page with a persistent consequence; if absent, document why and the next meaningful opportunity rather than forcing a quota. In every 20-chapter arc, plan at least one consequential non-game thread with setup, escalation, choice, payoff and aftermath, including other characters' independent struggles. A single event cannot be counted repeatedly without a genuinely changed consequence. Avoid an unrelieved chain of disasters: recoveries, pleasures, friendships, earned success and social humor matter. Do not reduce life outside the game to tournament logistics.
