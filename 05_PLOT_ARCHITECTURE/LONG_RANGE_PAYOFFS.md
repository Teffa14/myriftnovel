## Seed lifecycle
1. Establish a locally meaningful ordinary detail, action, relationship, object, preference or decision.
2. Develop an association through varied genuine experiences, reciprocal actions and independent motives.
3. Allow dormancy and natural change; do not repeat an object, gesture or phrase merely to remind readers of it.
4. Recontextualize through a consequential later scene that stands on its own for readers who have forgotten the original detail.
5. Show persistent consequences and aftermath rather than requiring a single scene to explain every connection.

Use a mix of nearby, medium-range, long-range and saga-scale payoffs. Proposed chapter ranges are flexible. Do not withhold all rewards for hundreds of chapters. Some details should remain ordinary and never become callbacks. Preserve the intrinsic pleasures of AD-0008. Build positive attachment before irreversible consequences; do not make every happy moment an omen.

## Dense scene planning and acceptance
Before a major emotional scene, identify independently established causal threads, viewpoint and reader knowledge, the immediate objective, competing commitments, the actual turning point, sensory or material consequences, relationship changes, and subsequent aftermath. Select callbacks based on evidence and narrative necessity. One precise callback may be stronger than a montage of references. Do not reuse retired language, prewrite compulsory farewell speeches, invent earlier promises, create melodrama by hiding facts a viewpoint knows, or imply that later achievement justifies personal suffering. Preserve independent character agency, chronology, realistic resources and the existing scoped competitive gates. When the scene involves actual game mechanics or real-world factual claims, verify those details before drafting.
