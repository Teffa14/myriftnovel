# Competitive integrity v2 — scoped verification, autonomous editorial production
Author instruction 2026-11-25: author supplies neither game logs nor rules expertise. The production AI owns source retrieval, construction, analysis and verification. This policy supersedes the old blanket G3 dependency across all prior documents. Never ask author to validate rules or provide replays as a routine prerequisite. No game software clone or public simulator is needed; only private editorial evidence and narrowly scoped checks.
 
## Separate gates
G3-global: complete card database, identity reconciliation, effective-text histories, executable full match engine and deck validation. Still incomplete; retain original coverage QA, do not declare passed. This is a development goal, not a prerequisite for writing every scene.
G3-scene: verify each mechanically meaningful claim actually depicted or relied upon by a scene, from dated official rules/card data; maintain internally consistent starting conditions, known vs assumed state, effects, legal options, information and consequences. This is mandatory before editorial acceptance of technical prose.
Levels:
- L0 narrative-only: fictional outcomes/relationships and generic competition; no specific rules interaction or unsupported claims about named cards. Verify any stated format, score or other concrete claim. No full replay needed. If outcome depends on a specific tactic, use L1 or L2.
- L1 bounded tactical segment: construct fiction from plausible stated premises; source every relevant rule/printed and corrected card text if named, resolve triggers/restrictions that may affect claim; prove the depicted transition and document its scope. Earlier off-page actions may remain unmodeled, but cannot be presented as validated; check plausibility and no known contradictory effect. If exact card effects cannot be checked, omit names/interaction rather than claim an exhaustive board. Rules reasoning and executed scoped arithmetic checks are distinct. Label evidence as conditional, not a full replay or certified judge ruling. A concrete legal alternative or optimality claim needs independent supporting evidence, not inference from a loss.
- L2 full replay: for extended technical duels, generate two plausible legal decklists with provenance, validate all card identities, restrictions and actual gameplay state from start to finish; source every effect, record draws, priority, actions, scoring and visibility. Reproduce with available validated tools or paper-style ledger and independent rules pass. Do not claim deterministic engine execution unless executed. If unavailable, narrow scope to L1 and rewrite the scene accordingly. Never assert omitted portions are checked.
 
## Autonomous workflow
1. Determine story date, region, format, POV and narrative purpose. Retrieve official Rules Hub, dated Core Rules, Tournament Rules when relevant, banlist, relevant FAQ and errata. Source priority: effective official card wording/rulings, rule documents, official gallery, then community decklists/data for discovery. Record URLs, dates, rule IDs, pages, and card codes. Verify each named card's relevant current wording and legal availability; a metadata row alone is insufficient. Unrelated missing printings cannot block a scoped scene.
2. Construct a fictional scenario with explicit starting assumptions. Use known real archetypes when sourcing permits; select plausible cards and resources, distinct strategic objectives, and only information each player could know. For a full match choose a seed or draw order before simulation; record it and do not reroll to force victory. Fictional outcomes are legitimate plot decisions; do not present constructed scenarios as real recordings or random simulations. An outline may target a loss; actual depicted moves must follow sourced rules.
3. Resolve in a separate rules pass: verify legality, timing, costs, effects, hidden/public knowledge, counters and scoring. Use executable checks wherever possible; run negative tests. If a card or interaction is uncertain, replace it with a verified simpler interaction or narrow the scene to known mechanics. Do not use concession as a universal fallback; preserve the strategic cause. Do not invent an alternative move or assert best play without a supported state.
4. Record claim-by-claim evidence and limitations in private competitive_replay.json (scope, not necessarily full replay), sources_used, scene_claims, premises, state/events, execution_report, unverified_claims, verdict. Use separate reviewer/agent if available; otherwise disclose same-agent review. No official judge certification claimed. For L1, any unverified detail must be removed from narration or explicitly treated as character hypothesis rather than fact, without relying on it for the mechanical outcome.
5. If source retrieval fails, try direct official pages, PDF rule search, local catalog/importers, available community data as index, or rewrite to a narrower verified scope. Continue independent scenes. Author approval is for story/quality, not rules adjudication. G6 and actual archive requirements remain unchanged. Do not claim inaccessible manuscript was recovered. 
6. Accept chapter only after G3-scene is supported for every technical claim, all other per-chapter gates satisfied, and explicit approval. Global G3 remains tracked separately. No public full-card text/art or simulator distribution; existing rights gate remains.
