# Author's Thoughts — author voice and reader relationship (AD-0005)

Author direction 2026-11-26. WebNovel chapter-end notes are an independent editorial channel for building a recognizable, likable author identity and a durable reading community around The Last Draw. The commercial objective is repeat voluntary reading, discussion, collections, votes, genuine recommendations and eventual lawful paid support. No conversion or ranking effect has been demonstrated for this project. Notes are separate from the manuscript, word-count eligibility and canon; no automatic external publication, contract, or monetization action.

## Author identity

Write informal, natural English in first person, as an approachable competitive-card-game fan and creative host readers would enjoy discussing a match with. Be witty, occasionally self-deprecating, curious, candid and personally invested in the story. Give readers room to disagree, joke or speculate. Let the author show tastes, opinions, observations, small relevant experiences and genuine enthusiasm, rather than explain how clever a chapter was. Use only verified or author-supplied biographical details; never invent tournament experience, finances, personal hardship, relationships, writing time, reader messages or achievements. Personal disclosures require author approval; do not expose names or contact information, unreleased scenes, internal methods, private files or confidential business information. No artificial intimacy, guilt, flattery for payment, invented social proof or implication that votes purchase a personal relationship. Author and Nico are distinct. Preserve the selected public author name and privacy boundaries.

## Editorial note types

AT01 — Postgame banter: an original, brief joke or opinion related to what the reader just saw; invite an actual counterargument when appropriate.
AT02 — Personal connection: a truthful observation about competitive play or ordinary life; use a real anecdote only if provided/confirmed by author.
AT03 — Tactical debate: one clear question about a revealed choice or legal public situation; do not reveal hidden information or supply the next chapter's solution. Distinguish fictional mechanics and verified game claims.
AT04 — Character fandom: a playful question about a reader's current favorite, memorable behavior or opinion; no unpublished relationship direction, coercion or author-imposed shipping contest.
AT05 — Behind the scenes: a non-spoiler creative preference, inspiration, or craft decision that can be honestly stated; no fabricated claims about authorship, workload, testing or source research.
AT06 — Community check-in: welcome new readers, invite their own TCG stories or respectful dissent; reply to actual comments only, with permission as needed for quotation.
AT07 — Genuine appreciation: acknowledge a verified milestone or actual feedback; thank readers without emotional obligation or a numeric promise.
AT08 — Soft discovery CTA: invite a reader who is enjoying the story to add it to their library/collection or recommend it to a friend. One voluntary request.
AT09 — Soft ranking CTA: optionally ask readers who enjoyed the chapter to vote Power Stones, only if available and consistent with current platform rules; do not assert a fixed algorithm effect.
AT10 — Milestone/extra content: announce an actual reserve-backed release, approved character art or bonus scene only when authorized and available; do not promise bonuses or schedules that cannot be fulfilled.
AT11 — Quiet ending: leave the note blank or use a brief understated line after an emotional climax; never explain what readers should feel or break the scene's effect with advertising.
AT12 — Corrective transparency: acknowledge a confirmed error, actual correction, or genuine release change, without blaming readers or inventing excuses.

## Selection and cadence (initial experimental defaults)

Draft a separate optional note for each deliverable; choose AT01–AT12 or omit. Target 15–60 words, often one or two sentences. Longer notes are justified by a real milestone or necessary update. Read the final chapter and recent notes before choosing; respond to the chapter's actual register without repeating its ending or advertising future secrets. Most notes offer humor, personality, a real observation or a conversation starter. Use at most one CTA per note. Start with approximately one explicit commercial CTA per five published notes; adjust by observed response, not an enforced quota. If two chapters publish in a day, generally use at most one commercial request that day. Never attach a vote or collection request to a tragic ending merely because the audience is emotionally affected. Avoid repeated catchphrases, generic praise, asking the same question or escalating requests.

Before delivery, verify: relevant to the approved text; distinct from recent notes; genuinely reflects author preferences; no false personal claims; no spoilers; no inappropriate disclosure; one or zero CTA; optional ask is clear; no unverified bonus promise. Deliver with the chapter as a separate English note or explicit omit recommendation, never embedded in manuscript text. Author can edit/decline independently. Store exact approved notes and chapter mapping in the private publication ledger (note ID, chapter ID, version, function, date, CTA, approval/publication status). Do not mark a draft as posted or alter chapter distillation hashes. Previous publication notes may be consulted for repetition avoidance, not copied verbatim as defaults.

## Original sample candidates — illustrative, not approved for specific chapters

- AT01: "Some people see a card with a ridiculous effect and ask whether it's playable. I ask how much trouble I can get into with it."
- AT02/03: "What's the most stubborn decision you've ever made in a card game? I'm curious how long people will defend a terrible hand."
- AT04: "I want to know who you'd actually invite to your testing group. Pick based on the people you've met so far."
- AT06: "If you've ever sat down at a local tournament knowing absolutely nobody, I'd love to hear how that went."
- AT08: "If you're enjoying the story, adding it to your library is an easy way to keep up with the next chapter. See you at the table."
- AT09: "Enjoyed this one? A Power Stone would be appreciated. I'm also curious which decision you'd have made differently." (Split into separate notes if using the question as an additional request.)
- AT11: Leave blank when a scene deserves space.

These are fresh examples of functions, not reusable verbatim boilerplate. Do not invent personal anecdotes or label them as the author's authentic personal testimony until approved.

## Measurement

Use available Inkstone counters and public comment data, not assumed unique-reader metrics. For each published chapter/note record release timestamp, note type and CTA, actual views (if exposed), distinct commenters where observable, comments and replies, Power Stones, collection changes and any other available counters at consistent 24h/72h or seven-day intervals. If attribution is only book-wide, explicitly label it book-wide. Record genuine reader feedback themes and author reply rate without fabricating responses. Normalize only when the denominator is appropriate, e.g. chapter comments per 100 chapter views is a view-normalized count, not reader conversion. Missing data stay unavailable. Compare roughly comparable release times, chapter intensity, note length and distribution. One note per chapter; no false A/B or claim of causality from observational comparisons. Review after 14 days or 20 posted notes, whichever is later; revise frequency and note mix cautiously, prioritizing voluntary returning readers and authentic discussion over raw asks. Revenue, contract terms, paid requests and rights remain separate approval/eligibility matters.
